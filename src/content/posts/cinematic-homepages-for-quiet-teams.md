A publication homepage has a hard job. It needs to create appetite, explain the editorial point of view, and move the reader toward a concrete story quickly. The strongest versions avoid the generic landing page pattern and start with the work itself.

For a Jekflix-inspired site, the hero is not decoration. It is the lead story, with enough cinematic weight to set the mood and enough metadata to make the next click feel low risk.

## What the hero needs

The first screen should answer three questions:

- What is the publication about?
- Which story should I start with?
- What else is available if that story is not for me?

That last question is why rails and grids matter. A strong hero with no nearby browsing context feels like a poster. A strong hero with visible related cards feels like a catalog.

## Keep the surface dense

Density does not mean clutter. It means every card earns its place. Category, reading time, author, and date are enough for most browsing decisions. Excerpts are useful when they are short and written as editorial hooks, not summaries.

The result should feel like a fast aisle scan: visual first, then title, then context.
